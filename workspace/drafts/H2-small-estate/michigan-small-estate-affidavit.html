The short version (2026): For deaths in 2026, Michigan’s small estate limit is $53,000 (a $50,000 base adjusted for inflation). If the person owned no real estate, an heir can use the PC 598 sworn statement at a bank 28 days after the death, with no court filing. If there is real estate, the probate court’s assignment of small estate can handle it, and up to $264,000 of a mortgage is subtracted from the home’s value. Vehicles up to $100,000 transfer separately through the Secretary of State.

The Michigan small estate affidavit, Form PC 598, is the fastest way to collect a modest estate in the state, and Michigan rebuilt the rules around it in 2024. Public Act 1 of 2024, in effect since February 21, 2024, raised the base limit for both small estate procedures to $50,000 and indexed it for inflation. It also let families subtract a mortgage when real estate is part of a court small estate case, which brings many modest homes within reach for the first time.

Michigan offers two procedures under the Estates and Protected Individuals Code (EPIC), and the law says no one other than the court can force you to use one instead of the other. This guide explains both, the 2026 numbers, and how to choose.

The 2026 Michigan numbers

  • Small estate limit: $53,000 for 2026 deaths. The statute sets $50,000, adjusted each year under MCL 700.1210.
  • Mortgage deduction for real estate (court route only): up to $264,000 for 2026 deaths, from a $250,000 base.
  • Waiting period for the affidavit: 28 days after the death.
  • Vehicles: heirs can transfer vehicles worth up to $100,000 using a Secretary of State form, outside both procedures.

Because the limit is indexed, always use the figure for the year of death. Michigan courts publish the adjusted amounts each year.

Before 2024, Michigan’s small estate figure was far lower, and a mortgaged house almost always forced a full probate case. The 2024 law changed both points at once. If a relative died before February 21, 2024, check with the probate court which version of the rules applies, because the older limits and the older real estate treatment may still control that estate.

Route 1: The PC 598 affidavit (no court)

MCL 700.3983 lets a successor collect property with a sworn statement when:

  1. The estate includes no real property;
  2. The value of the entire estate, wherever located, net of liens and encumbrances, does not exceed the adjusted limit ($53,000 for 2026 deaths);
  3. 28 days have passed since the death; and
  4. No application or petition to appoint a personal representative is pending or has been granted anywhere.

The sworn statement must also say that the person claiming is entitled to the property and list the name, address and share of every other person entitled. The State Court Administrative Office publishes the standard form, PC 598, which carries a perjury warning.

Present the signed PC 598 and a death certificate to the bank, employer or other holder. Holders must pay or deliver, and transfer agents must re-register securities in the successor’s name. Both heirs and the person who paid the funeral bill can use this route.

Note that the affidavit measures value net of liens: a $20,000 boat with a $12,000 loan counts as $8,000.

PC 598 is the official form, but banks and brokerages sometimes ask for supporting sworn statements about the family or the assets. LawDepot’s affidavit builder helps you prepare clean, notarizable statements for those requests.

Route 2: Assignment of small estate (probate court)

MCL 700.3982 lets the probate court order property turned over without a full estate when the funeral and burial expenses are handled and the balance of the gross estate is $53,000 or less (for 2026 deaths):

  • If the funeral has been paid by the estate, the court may order the remaining property turned over to the surviving spouse, or if there is none, to the heirs.
  • If the funeral is unpaid, or was paid by someone else, the court orders the property used first to pay or reimburse the funeral and burial expenses, and may then turn over the balance.

This is the only Michigan small estate procedure that works when the decedent owned real property. Since January 1, 2024, when real estate in the estate secures a debt, the debt is subtracted from the property’s value, up to the adjusted cap ($264,000 for 2026 deaths).

The 63-day rule. An heir who receives property under a court assignment (other than a surviving spouse who qualifies for allowances, or the decedent’s minor children) is responsible for 63 days after the order for any unsatisfied debt of the decedent, up to the value received. The order must state this condition.

For an estate with only bank accounts and belongings, the PC 598 affidavit is almost always faster. When the decedent owned a house, even a heavily mortgaged one, the court assignment is the tool to check first.

A worked example

A Grand Rapids woman died in 2026 without a will, leaving two adult sons. She owned, in her own name, a house worth $210,000 with a $165,000 mortgage, a checking account with $6,000, and a car worth $9,000. Her funeral was paid by her older son.

  • Car: the sons transfer it through the Secretary of State form for vehicles up to $100,000. It does not need either procedure.
  • PC 598 is out: the estate includes real property.
  • Court assignment: the house counts at $210,000 minus the $165,000 mortgage (under the $264,000 cap) = $45,000. Add the $6,000 checking account for a gross estate of $51,000, then account for the funeral paid by the older son.

Because the balance after funeral expenses is under $53,000, the sons can petition the probate court for an assignment. The court orders the funeral reimbursed first and the rest, including the house subject to its mortgage, turned over to the two sons, who remain responsible for 63 days for any unpaid debts up to what they received.

What counts toward the $53,000

Both procedures measure only the probate estate: property in the decedent’s sole name with no built-in transfer at death. Leave out:

  • Joint accounts and jointly owned property with right of survivorship, which belong to the survivor;
  • Payable-on-death and transfer-on-death bank and brokerage accounts;
  • Life insurance, pensions, 401(k)s and IRAs with a living beneficiary;
  • Trust assets, which the trustee handles; and
  • Real estate passing by a ladybird deed (enhanced life estate deed) or joint tenancy, which transfers outside the estate.

Vehicles are handled through the Secretary of State process up to $100,000, so most families set the car aside before running the numbers.

Common mistakes with Michigan small estates

  • Using last year’s limit. Michigan indexes the figure every year. A 2025 death and a 2026 death can have different limits.
  • Filing PC 598 when there is real estate. Even a small parcel or a mortgaged house rules out the affidavit. Use the court assignment instead.
  • Measuring the wrong way. The affidavit uses value net of liens; the court assignment uses gross value after funeral expenses, with only real-estate debt subtracted.
  • Leaving out other heirs. PC 598 requires the name, address and share of every other person entitled. A signer who leaves someone out risks perjury and personal liability.
  • Distributing before debts are clear. After a court assignment, heirs remain answerable for 63 days for unpaid debts up to what they received.

When the estate is over $53,000

If neither route fits, Michigan probate follows, usually as an unsupervised (informal) administration through the probate register. Check first whether assets passed outside the estate: joint accounts, payable-on-death accounts, beneficiary designations, trust assets, and real estate covered by a Michigan ladybird deed or joint tenancy. How Michigan compares

At $53,000, Michigan’s limit is below the national middle value of $75,000, but its 2024 reforms put it ahead of most states on real estate: few states let a small estate procedure reach a mortgaged home at all. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Michigan small estate limit for 2026?

$53,000 for deaths in 2026. The statute sets a $50,000 base that is adjusted every year for inflation, so use the figure for the year the person died.

Can I use a Michigan small estate affidavit if there is a house?

Not the PC 598 affidavit; it requires that the estate include no real property. The probate court’s assignment of small estate can include real estate, and a mortgage on the home is subtracted from its value up to $264,000 for 2026 deaths.

How long do I have to wait to use PC 598 in Michigan?

28 days after the death. The sworn statement must say that 28 days have passed and that no personal representative has been appointed or applied for.

Do I need to file PC 598 with the court?

No. You give the signed PC 598 and a death certificate directly to the bank or other holder. The court assignment under section 3982 is the procedure that requires a probate court filing.

How do I transfer a car in Michigan without probate?

Heirs can transfer vehicles worth up to $100,000 through the Michigan Secretary of State using its form for transfers after death, without either small estate procedure. Jointly owned vehicles transfer to the surviving owner with the title, a death certificate and identification.

Am I liable for the decedent’s debts after a court assignment?

For 63 days after the order, an heir who receives property is responsible for unpaid debts of the decedent up to the value received. A qualifying surviving spouse and the decedent’s minor children are exempt from that rule.
