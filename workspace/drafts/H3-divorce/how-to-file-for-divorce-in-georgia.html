The short version (2027): Here is how to file for divorce in Georgia in brief: one spouse must have been a Georgia resident for 6 months before filing. Georgia allows a no-fault divorce when the marriage is irretrievably broken, but the court cannot grant it until at least 30 days after service on the other spouse. Filing costs $220 in Fulton County, plus $50 if the sheriff serves the papers. You file in the Superior Court of the county where the other spouse lives, in most cases. Georgia divides marital property equitably, and adultery or desertion that caused the separation can bar a spouse from receiving alimony.

Georgia is one of the faster states for an agreed divorce. Once the 30-day period after service has passed, a judge can sign an uncontested decree, often without a hearing, if the spouses have a signed settlement agreement. Georgia also has a few rules that surprise people: you generally file in the other spouse’s county, fault still matters for alimony, and parents must file a parenting plan and usually attend a parenting seminar.

This guide explains how to file for divorce in Georgia step by step: residency and venue, grounds, the 30-day rule, fees, forms, property, alimony and children. Georgia Residency Requirement: 6 Months

Under O.C.G.A. §19-5-2, no court can grant a divorce to a person who has not been a bona fide resident of Georgia for six months before filing. Two special rules apply:

  • Military. A person who has lived on a U.S. Army post or military reservation in Georgia for one year before filing can file in any county next to the post or reservation.
  • Nonresident filers. A spouse who lives outside Georgia can file against a spouse who has lived in Georgia, and in that county, for six months, in the county where the resident spouse lives.

Where to File: Usually the Other Spouse’s County

Georgia’s Constitution generally requires civil cases to be filed in the county where the defendant lives. For divorce, that means you usually file in the Superior Court of the county where your spouse lives. If your spouse has moved out of Georgia, you can file in your own county. If your spouse consents, many courts accept the case in the plaintiff’s county with a signed acknowledgment of venue.

Grounds for Divorce in Georgia

O.C.G.A. §19-5-3 lists 13 grounds. The no-fault ground is number 13: the marriage is irretrievably broken. Almost all uncontested Georgia divorces use it.

The other twelve are fault grounds, including adultery, desertion for one year, cruel treatment, habitual intoxication, and conviction of a crime of moral turpitude with a sentence of two years or more. Fault is rarely needed to get the divorce, but it can matter for alimony and property.

The 30-Day Rule

For a divorce on the no-fault ground, §19-5-3(13) says that “under no circumstances” shall the court grant a divorce until not less than 30 days from the date of service on the respondent. The clock starts at service, not filing, so the fastest route is for your spouse to sign an acknowledgment of service as soon as you file.

The other spouse also has 30 days after service to file an answer. If the spouses have a signed settlement agreement, many Georgia judges will sign the final decree on the papers soon after day 31. Contested divorces commonly take six months to a year or more.

What It Costs to File for Divorce in Georgia

Superior court clerks set fees under state law, and they are similar across the state. The Fulton County Clerk of Superior Court’s fee schedule lists:

Item (Fulton County Superior Court)Fee
Divorce filing$220
Sheriff service fee (per service)$50

If your spouse signs an acknowledgment of service, you do not pay the sheriff. Many circuits also charge for the required parenting seminar if you have children.

Fee waiver. If you cannot afford the costs, file an affidavit of indigence (sometimes called a pauper’s affidavit) with your petition. The court can allow you to proceed without paying the filing fee.

Georgia Divorce Forms

Georgia’s courts and legal aid organizations publish free divorce packets (with and without children), and many superior courts have a family law information center. A typical uncontested case uses:

  • Petition for Divorce (verified);
  • Domestic Relations Case Filing Information Form, required with every new case;
  • Acknowledgment of Service and Consent to Jurisdiction and Venue signed by the other spouse, or a summons for sheriff service;
  • Domestic Relations Financial Affidavit from each spouse;
  • Settlement Agreement, and with children a Parenting Plan and Child Support Worksheet;
  • Final Judgment and Decree of Divorce; and
  • the Report of Divorce form for vital records.

How to File for Divorce in Georgia, Step by Step

  1. Confirm residency (six months) and the right county, usually where your spouse lives.
  2. Agree on the terms and put them in a signed settlement agreement; with children, add a parenting plan and child support worksheet.
  3. File the petition with the Clerk of Superior Court, in person or through the county’s e-filing system, and pay the fee or file an affidavit of indigence.
  4. Serve your spouse: have them sign an acknowledgment of service before a notary, or have the sheriff serve them.
  5. Complete the parenting seminar if you have children and your circuit requires it.
  6. Wait at least 30 days after service.
  7. Submit the final decree with the settlement agreement and financial affidavits. Some judges sign on the papers; others set a short uncontested hearing.
  8. Get certified copies of the signed decree.

Property: Equitable Division

Georgia is an equitable division state. Property acquired during the marriage through either spouse’s efforts is marital property and is divided fairly, which is not always equally. Property owned before the marriage, and gifts and inheritances received by one spouse, are generally separate property. If the case goes to trial, a jury can decide the property division in Georgia, which is rare among states.

Alimony and Fault

Georgia allows temporary and permanent alimony, based on the needs of one spouse and the other’s ability to pay (O.C.G.A. §19-6-1 and §19-6-5). Fault matters here: under §19-6-1(b), a spouse is not entitled to alimony if the separation was caused by that spouse’s adultery or desertion. Georgia has no statutory formula for amount or duration. Divorce With Children in Georgia

  • Parenting plan. Georgia requires a parenting plan in every case involving custody (O.C.G.A. §19-9-1). Our parenting plan template covers the usual terms.
  • Child’s preference. A child aged 14 or older can choose which parent to live with, subject to the judge’s best-interest review; the judge considers the wishes of a child aged 11 to 13.
  • Child support. Georgia uses an income shares model with a statewide worksheet (§19-6-15).
  • Parenting seminar. Most circuits require both parents to complete a seminar before the decree.

Separate Maintenance: Georgia’s Version of Legal Separation

Georgia does not use the term “legal separation,” but it offers an action for separate maintenance. A spouse who is living apart can ask the court to order support, divide responsibility for debts, and set custody and child support without ending the marriage. People choose it for religious reasons, to keep a spouse on a health insurance plan, or because they are not yet sure about divorce. A separate maintenance order does not end the marriage, so neither spouse can remarry. If you later decide to divorce, the separate maintenance order can guide the final settlement. After the Decree: Name Change and Records

A Georgia divorce decree can restore a former or maiden name if you ask for it in the petition or settlement agreement, which saves a separate name change case later. After the judge signs the decree, get several certified copies from the clerk. You will need them to update your driver’s license and Social Security record, to change the names on bank accounts, vehicle titles and deeds, and to send a retirement division order to a plan administrator. Also review beneficiary designations on life insurance and retirement accounts; a divorce does not automatically change all of them.

Online Divorce in Georgia

Most Georgia superior courts accept electronic filing through approved e-filing providers, and legal aid organizations publish free, fillable uncontested divorce packets. Paid online services prepare the same forms and can help couples who have a detailed property list. Our guide on filing for an uncontested divorce online explains when a service is worth the cost.

Common Mistakes in Georgia Divorces

  • Filing in the wrong county. The petition usually belongs in the other spouse’s county unless they have left Georgia or consent to venue.
  • Counting from filing. The 30 days run from service, so delay in service delays the decree.
  • Leaving out the case filing information form or the financial affidavits.
  • Skipping the parenting seminar. The judge may not sign until both parents finish it.
  • Ignoring the alimony bar. If adultery or desertion caused the separation, the at-fault spouse may lose the right to alimony, which can change settlement talks.

How Georgia Compares

Georgia’s 30-day wait after service is shorter than Texas (60 days) and far shorter than California (six months). Its rule that fault can bar alimony is stricter than in no-fault-only states such as Illinois. See all 50 states in our divorce requirements by state table.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you have to live in Georgia to file for divorce?

Six months as a bona fide Georgia resident before filing (O.C.G.A. §19-5-2). A spouse living outside Georgia can file in the other spouse’s county if that spouse has lived in Georgia and in that county for six months.

How long does an uncontested divorce take in Georgia?

At least 30 days after service. Many uncontested divorces with a signed settlement agreement are final within 31 to 60 days after service, depending on the court.

How much does it cost to file for divorce in Georgia?

In Fulton County, $220 to file plus $50 if the sheriff serves your spouse. Other counties charge similar amounts. You can file an affidavit of indigence if you cannot afford the fees.

Where do I file for divorce in Georgia?

Usually in the Superior Court of the county where your spouse lives. If your spouse lives outside Georgia, you can file in your own county.

Does adultery affect divorce in Georgia?

You do not need to prove fault to get divorced, but a spouse whose adultery or desertion caused the separation is not entitled to alimony (§19-6-1(b)).

Do I have to go to court for a Georgia divorce?

Not always. Many judges sign an uncontested decree on the papers when a complete settlement agreement is filed; others hold a short hearing.
