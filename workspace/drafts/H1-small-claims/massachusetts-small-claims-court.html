The short version (2026): Massachusetts small claims court handles claims up to $7,000 in the District Court, the Boston Municipal Court and the Housing Court, with no limit for property damage caused by a motor vehicle (G.L. c. 218, §21). Filing fees are $40 to $150 by claim size. The clerk notifies the defendant by first-class mail. By choosing small claims, the plaintiff gives up the right to appeal to a jury; the defendant can ask for a new trial within 10 days.

Massachusetts calls its small claims process a “simple, informal and inexpensive procedure” for contract and tort claims. Cases are often heard by a clerk-magistrate, mediation is offered when the case starts, and the court can order a losing party to pay in installments. The state also has one of the strongest consumer protection laws in the country, Chapter 93A, which many small claims plaintiffs can use.

This guide covers the Massachusetts small claims limit, fees, where to file, the hearing, appeals, payment orders and consumer claims. Rules link to the Massachusetts General Laws and the Trial Court’s small claims standards, checked in September 2026.

The Massachusetts Limit: $7,000

Under G.L. c. 218, §21, the small claims procedure applies to claims in contract or tort (other than slander and libel) where the plaintiff claims no more than $7,000. There are two important exceptions:

  • No dollar limit for property damage caused by a motor vehicle. Car accident property damage claims can be brought in small claims at any amount.
  • Cities and towns can use the procedure for certain tax collection actions and other claims up to $15,000.

If your claim is larger than $7,000 and not a motor vehicle damage claim, you can reduce it and give up the difference, or file a regular civil action by summons and complaint.

Where to File

Under G.L. c. 218, §21, the plaintiff can choose to file in the judicial district where either the plaintiff or the defendant lives or has a usual place of business or employment. A tenant suing a landlord over a residential rental can also file where the property is located. Small claims sessions are held in every District Court, the Boston Municipal Court, and the Housing Court.

How to File a Massachusetts Small Claim, Step by Step

  1. Send a demand letter. Use a demand letter for payment. If your claim involves an unfair or deceptive business practice, send a Chapter 93A demand at least 30 days before filing (see below).
  2. Check the deadline. Massachusetts generally allows six years for contract claims and three years for most tort claims; see our statute of limitations on debt by state table.
  3. Complete the Statement of Small Claim and Notice of Trial. A concise written statement of your claim is all that is required (G.L. c. 218, §22). Use the defendant’s full legal name and current address.
  4. File and pay. File in person, by mail or through eFileMA, and pay the fee for your claim size.
  5. Notice to the defendant. The court mails the notice of claim and trial date to the defendant by first-class mail.
  6. Consider mediation. When you file, you are told the case can be mediated if both sides agree. A settlement reached in mediation is noted by the clerk-magistrate and entered as a judgment.
  7. Attend the hearing. Bring documents and witnesses.

At the Hearing

A small claims case that does not settle is usually heard by a clerk-magistrate, sometimes by a judge. The hearing is informal, but the decision is made according to the rules of substantive law. Tips:

  • Open with the amount and the reason in one or two sentences.
  • Bring the original documents plus copies for the magistrate and the other side.
  • For car accident claims, bring repair estimates, photos and the crash report.
  • Bring witnesses in person if you can.

If the defendant does not appear, you can ask for a default judgment. If you do not appear, your claim may be dismissed. If you need an interpreter or a disability accommodation, tell the clerk’s office well before the hearing date.

Appeals: The Plaintiff Gives Up the Right; the Defendant Has 10 Days

Massachusetts has a one-sided appeal rule under G.L. c. 218, §23:

  • A plaintiff who starts a case in small claims is deemed to have waived trial by jury and any right of appeal to a jury.
  • The defendant may, within 10 days after receiving the magistrate’s finding, file a claim for a trial by a jury of six or by a single justice, with an affidavit stating that there are genuine questions of law and fact and that the trial is sought in good faith. If the defendant does so, the plaintiff can also claim a jury.

Small claims cases cannot be removed to the Superior Court. If you are the plaintiff and appeal rights matter to you, file a regular civil action instead of using small claims.

Payment Orders and Collection

Massachusetts courts can do more than enter a judgment. Under G.L. c. 218, §22, the court may, after inquiry, order the losing party to pay the amount due by a certain date or in installments, modify that order, and enforce it through contempt proceedings. If the debtor does not pay, you can ask for a payment review hearing (supplementary process), where the debtor must explain their finances under oath.

Other tools include:

  • Wage attachment (trustee process). Massachusetts protects most wages: the exempt amount each week is the greater of 85% of gross wages or 50 times the higher of the federal or Massachusetts minimum wage (G.L. c. 246, §28).
  • Execution. After judgment, the court can issue an execution that a sheriff or constable can use to seize non-exempt property.
  • Interest. Money judgments carry interest at the rate set by Massachusetts law, generally 12% a year in contract and tort cases.

Consumer Claims Under Chapter 93A

Massachusetts’ Consumer Protection Act, G.L. c. 93A, lets consumers recover damages for unfair or deceptive business practices, and in some cases double or triple damages plus attorney fees. Before filing, the consumer must mail or deliver a written demand for relief at least 30 days before suing, identifying the claimant and describing the unfair act and the injury (G.L. c. 93A, §9(3)). A business that makes a reasonable written settlement offer within 30 days can limit what it pays if the offer is rejected. Multiple damages and attorney fees awarded under Chapter 93A are generally treated as outside the $7,000 cap, so ask the clerk how to present them on the statement of claim.

Common Massachusetts Small Claims Cases

  • Security deposits. Landlords must follow strict rules under G.L. c. 186, §15B, including returning the deposit with interest within 30 days after the tenancy ends. Violations can lead to three times the amount plus costs and attorney fees. Start with a security deposit demand letter.
  • Car accident property damage. No dollar limit in small claims.
  • Contractor disputes. Contracts, payments, photos and a second estimate; a Chapter 93A claim may apply.
  • Personal loans. A promissory note, texts or bank transfers.

Common Mistakes in Massachusetts Small Claims

  • Skipping the 93A letter. Without a proper demand at least 30 days before filing, you may lose the chance to recover multiple damages under Chapter 93A.
  • Choosing small claims when you might want to appeal. The plaintiff gives up the right to a jury appeal by filing in small claims.
  • Using the wrong defendant name. Check the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s corporations database for a business’s exact name.
  • Ignoring the payment order. If the court orders installments and the debtor stops paying, go back promptly and ask for a payment review hearing.
  • Forgetting the motor vehicle exception. Car accident property damage claims are not capped at $7,000, so do not reduce them unnecessarily.

How Massachusetts Compares

Massachusetts’ $7,000 limit is below the most common national figure of $10,000, but its unlimited motor vehicle damage claims, mailed notice and Chapter 93A remedies make it more powerful than the number suggests. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the small claims limit in Massachusetts?

$7,000, with no limit for property damage caused by a motor vehicle (G.L. c. 218, §21).

How much does it cost to file small claims in Massachusetts?

$40 for claims of $500 or less, $50 up to $2,000, $100 up to $5,000 and $150 up to $7,000 or for larger motor vehicle damage claims.

Can the plaintiff appeal a Massachusetts small claims decision?

No. A plaintiff who files in small claims waives the right to appeal to a jury. The defendant can ask for a jury or single-justice trial within 10 days.

Where do I file a small claim in Massachusetts?

In the District Court, Boston Municipal Court or Housing Court for the area where either you or the defendant lives or works, or where a residential rental property is located.

Do I need to send a demand letter?

It is strongly recommended, and it is required at least 30 days before suing if you bring a consumer protection claim under Chapter 93A.

How much of a debtor’s wages can be taken in Massachusetts?

The debtor keeps the greater of 85% of gross wages or 50 times the higher of the federal or state minimum wage each week.
