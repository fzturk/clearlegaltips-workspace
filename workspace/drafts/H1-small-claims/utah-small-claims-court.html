The short version (2026): Utah small claims court hears money claims up to $20,000, including any attorney fees but not court costs or interest (Utah Code §78A-8-102). Cases are usually filed in a justice court. Filing fees are $60, $100 or $185 by claim size. Some courts use Online Dispute Resolution (ODR). The defendant must be served at least 30 days before trial (or within 120 days of filing in ODR cases), and either side can appeal within 28 days for a new trial in district court.

Utah has one of the highest small claims limits in the country and one of the most modern systems. Since 2025, claims up to $20,000 can be brought in small claims, and many courts let the parties try to resolve the case online, on their own schedule, before any hearing. The Utah courts’ self-help center publishes clear step-by-step instructions and forms.

This guide covers the Utah small claims limit, fees, where to file, ODR, service deadlines, the trial, appeals and collection. Rules link to the Utah Code, the Utah Rules of Small Claims Procedure and the Utah State Courts, checked in September 2026.

The Utah Limit: $20,000

According to the Utah State Courts, the most you can ask for in small claims is $20,000. That amount includes lawyer fees but does not include court costs or interest (Utah Code §78A-8-102). Under the statute’s schedule, the limit rose from $15,000 to $20,000 on January 1, 2025, and it stays at $20,000 through December 31, 2029.

Utah small claims courts can only award money. They cannot order someone to do something, such as return property. You also cannot sue a government agency or evict someone in small claims. If a car accident caused both property damage and personal injury, the courts explain that you may need to decide how to split the claims carefully, because filing two cases means one case does not decide the other.

Where to File

Small claims cases in Utah are usually filed in a justice court (Cache County is an exception for some locations, where cases go to district court). The Utah courts give you two options:

  • where the defendant lives, or
  • where the events happened.

Some cities and counties share justice courts, and the courts publish a table showing where to file instead. If you are suing a business, use the Utah Department of Commerce business search to find where the business is located and who its registered agent is.

Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)

Some Utah courts use Online Dispute Resolution under Utah Supreme Court Standing Order No. 13. In an ODR case, the parties use a phone or computer to exchange information and try to settle with the help of a facilitator, on their own time, before a trial is scheduled. If they cannot settle, the case moves to a hearing. The court’s instructions tell you whether your court uses ODR and which forms to file.

How to File a Utah Small Claim, Step by Step

  1. Ask for payment in writing. Send a demand letter and keep proof of delivery.
  2. Check the deadline. Utah generally allows six years for written contracts and four years for oral contracts; see our statute of limitations on debt by state table.
  3. Decide where to file using the options above.
  4. Complete the forms. Find out whether your court uses ODR, then fill out the Small Claims Affidavit and Summons (or the ODR version). The filing fee is shown on the cover sheet.
  5. File and pay. File with the justice court and pay the fee, or request a fee waiver.
  6. Serve the defendant. Under Rule 3 of the Utah Rules of Small Claims Procedure, in non-ODR cases the defendant must be served at least 30 days before the trial. In ODR cases, service must be completed within 120 days of filing. File proof of service with the court.
  7. Prepare for trial (or participate in ODR).

At Trial

Small claims trials in Utah are informal and are held before a justice court judge. The judge decides who wins and tells the parties. Tips:

  • Lead with the amount and the reason.
  • Bring originals and copies of contracts, invoices, receipts, photos and messages.
  • Bring witnesses in person, or ask the clerk about a subpoena if someone will not come voluntarily.
  • If you are claiming attorney fees under a contract, remember they count toward the $20,000 limit.

Lawyers are allowed in Utah small claims, though most individuals represent themselves.

Appeals: A Trial De Novo in District Court

If you lose and think the judge made a mistake, you can appeal within 28 days of the judgment. You file the appeal forms with the court that issued the judgment, which sends the case to the nearest district court in the same judicial district. The district court holds a trial de novo, hearing the case again from the beginning. The appeal fee is $240, plus $10 payable to the justice court when you file the notice of appeal.

Collecting a Utah Judgment

If the defendant does not pay, the Utah courts’ “How to Collect a Judgment” guide walks through the options:

  • Supplemental proceedings. The debtor must appear and answer questions about income and assets.
  • Wage garnishment. For ordinary debts, Utah follows the federal cap: no more than 25% of disposable earnings, or the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage if less.
  • Bank garnishment and writs of execution. Reach non-exempt bank funds and property.
  • Judgment lien. Recording the judgment creates a lien on the debtor’s real estate in the county.

Interest. Utah post-judgment interest equals the federal post-judgment interest rate as of January 1 of the year the judgment is entered, plus 2 percentage points, and that rate stays fixed for the life of the judgment (Utah Code §15-1-4), unless a contract sets a different rate. The Utah State Courts publish each year’s rate.

Small Claims or District Court?

With a $20,000 limit, many Utah disputes that would need a regular lawsuit in other states can stay in small claims. Small claims is usually the better choice when you want a quick, low-cost hearing and can present the case yourself. District court makes more sense if you need the court to order someone to do something (like return property), if your claim is above $20,000 once attorney fees are included, or if you need formal discovery. Because the loser can ask for a trial de novo in district court, a well-prepared small claims case is also good practice for a second hearing if it comes to that.

Before You File: Can You Collect?

Winning a large judgment only helps if the defendant can pay. Under the federal 25% cap, a debtor with $900 a week in disposable earnings could have up to about $225 a week garnished, so a $15,000 judgment would take well over a year to collect from wages alone. Before filing, write down what you know about the defendant’s employer, bank, vehicles and real estate. If collection looks difficult, an ODR settlement with a realistic payment schedule may be worth more than a bigger judgment.

Common Utah Small Claims Cases

  • Security deposits. Utah landlords must return the deposit, with a written itemization of any deductions, within 30 days after the tenancy ends or within 15 days after receiving the tenant’s forwarding address, whichever is later. Start with a security deposit demand letter.
  • Car accident property damage. Repair estimates, photos and the crash report.
  • Personal loans. A promissory note, texts or bank transfers.
  • Contractor disputes. Contracts, payments, photos and a second estimate.

Common Mistakes in Utah Small Claims

  • Using an old limit. Charts showing $11,000 or $15,000 are out of date; the limit is $20,000 through 2029.
  • Forgetting that attorney fees count. If you claim contract attorney fees, they are part of the $20,000.
  • Serving too late. Non-ODR cases require service at least 30 days before trial.
  • Asking for the wrong relief. Small claims can award money only, not the return of property.
  • Missing the 28-day appeal window. It runs from the judgment.

How Utah Compares

Utah’s $20,000 limit ties Minnesota, Texas and West Virginia for the third-highest in the country, behind only Delaware and Tennessee at $25,000. It is far above neighbors Colorado ($7,500) and Arizona ($5,000). See every state in our small claims court limits by state table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the small claims limit in Utah in 2026?

$20,000, including attorney fees but not court costs or interest. The limit applies through December 31, 2029.

How much does it cost to file small claims in Utah?

$60 for claims of $2,000 or less, $100 for claims over $2,000 and under $7,500, and $185 for claims of $7,500 to $20,000, plus service costs.

What is Online Dispute Resolution in Utah small claims?

In courts that use it, ODR lets the parties exchange information and try to settle online, with a facilitator, before a trial is scheduled.

How far in advance must the defendant be served?

At least 30 days before trial in regular cases, or within 120 days of filing in ODR cases.

Can I appeal a Utah small claims decision?

Yes, within 28 days of the judgment. The district court holds a new trial. The fee is $240 plus $10 to the justice court.

Can I sue to get property back in Utah small claims?

No. Utah small claims courts can only award money. They cannot order the return of property.
