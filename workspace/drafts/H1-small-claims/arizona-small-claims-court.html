The short version (2026): Arizona small claims court, the small claims division of each justice court, now hears claims up to $5,000, up from $3,500 after Senate Bill 1022 (2025). In Maricopa County the filing fee is $58. Lawyers cannot take part, the defendant has 20 days to answer, and the decision is final with no appeal. Anyone who wants to keep appeal rights must move the case to the regular justice court at least 10 days before the hearing.

Arizona’s small claims division is built around one big trade-off. It is quick, cheap and lawyer-free, but the decision is final: neither side can appeal. Because of that, Arizona lets either party move the case to the regular justice court civil docket before the hearing, and the complaint form warns everyone about it in bold type.

This guide covers the new $5,000 limit, fees, who can represent a business, the answer and hearing timeline, the no-appeal rule and how to collect. Rules link to Title 22, Chapter 5, Article 1 of the Arizona Revised Statutes and the Maricopa County Justice Courts, checked in September 2026.

The Arizona Limit: $5,000 (Raised in 2025)

Under A.R.S. §22-503(A), the small claims division has concurrent jurisdiction with the justice court over civil actions where the debt, damage, tort, injury or value of personal property claimed by the plaintiff or the defendant does not exceed $5,000, exclusive of interest and costs. It can also hear actions to cancel or rescind a contract, or for equitable relief, up to $5,000. Senate Bill 1022 (Laws 2025, chapter 94) raised the limit from $3,500.

Small claims cannot hear, among other things (§22-503(B)):

  • defamation (libel or slander);
  • evictions (forcible entry, forcible or unlawful detainer);
  • actions for specific performance; or
  • class actions.

Claims above $5,000 up to the justice court’s general civil limit can be filed on the regular justice court docket, where lawyers and appeals are allowed.

Who Can File, and Who Represents Whom

Under A.R.S. §22-512, any person, corporation, partnership, association, marital community or other organization can sue or defend, but an assignee or other person who was not a party to the original transaction cannot bring a small claims case. Representation is tightly controlled:

  • An individual represents himself or herself.
  • Either spouse (or both) can represent a marital community.
  • A partnership appears through an active general partner or authorized full-time employee.
  • A corporation appears through a full-time officer or authorized employee.
  • An attorney may not appear or take any part in filing, prosecuting or defending a small claim (§22-512(B)(7)).

How to File an Arizona Small Claim, Step by Step

  1. Ask for payment in writing. Send a demand letter and keep proof of delivery.
  2. Check the deadline. Arizona generally allows six years for written contracts and three years for oral agreements; see our statute of limitations on debt by state table.
  3. Find the right justice court precinct. Maricopa County alone has 26 precincts. The correct one generally depends on where the transaction or agreement happened or where the defendant lives.
  4. File the Small Claims Complaint, Summons and Notice. Pay the filing fee.
  5. Serve the defendant. Use a constable, sheriff or certified private process server.
  6. Wait for the answer. The summons gives the defendant 20 days from service to answer (§22-514).
  7. Hearing date. Once an answer is filed, the clerk sets a hearing within 60 days and notifies the parties (§22-515).
  8. Decide about transfer. Either party can move the case to the regular justice court by objecting at least 10 days before the hearing (§22-504).
  9. Attend the hearing. Bring your evidence and witnesses. A party who fails to appear risks a judgment against them.

At the Hearing

A justice of the peace or a hearing officer (often a volunteer attorney) decides the case. Under §22-516, they must conduct the trial to do justice between the parties and are not bound by formal rules of procedure, pleading or evidence, except for privileged communications. Any material, relevant and competent evidence may be admitted. There is no discovery: you cannot send interrogatories or take depositions.

  • Bring the original and copies of contracts, invoices, receipts, photos and messages.
  • Show how you calculated the amount.
  • Bring witnesses in person if you can.

The written judgment must be issued within 10 days after the hearing and mailed to all parties (§22-520). It is due and payable immediately.

No Appeal, So Decide Before the Hearing

A.R.S. §22-519 is blunt: “There shall be no appeal in a small claims procedure,” and the decision is final and binding on both parties. The complaint form must warn you of this in bold type and explain how to keep your appeal rights.

If you want the option to appeal, or to have a lawyer, file on the regular justice court docket from the start, or have the case transferred at least 10 days before the hearing under §22-504. The regular justice court has more formal rules and higher fees, but its judgments can be appealed to the superior court.

Collecting an Arizona Judgment

Collection follows the general justice court procedures (§22-520(D)):

  • Judgment debtor exam. The debtor must answer questions under oath about income and assets ($33 in Maricopa County).
  • Wage garnishment, with a low cap. Since the 2022 voter-approved Predatory Debt Collection Protection Act, Arizona limits ordinary wage garnishment to the lesser of 10% of disposable earnings or the amount by which weekly disposable earnings exceed 60 times the highest applicable minimum wage (A.R.S. §33-1131).
  • Bank garnishment and writ of execution. Reach non-exempt bank funds and property.
  • Judgment lien. Recording the judgment with the county recorder creates a lien on the debtor’s real estate in that county.

Interest. Unless a statute or written contract says otherwise, judgments bear interest at the lesser of 10% a year or the prime rate plus 1% (A.R.S. §44-1201(B)). Medical debt judgments have their own rules.

Small Claims or the Regular Justice Court Docket?

For claims of $5,000 or less you can choose either the small claims division or the regular justice court civil docket. Small claims is the better fit when you want a quick, informal hearing, you are comfortable presenting your own evidence, and you accept that the result is final. The regular docket makes more sense if you want a lawyer, if the other side is likely to hire one, if you need discovery to get documents from the defendant, or if you want the ability to appeal. Keep in mind that either side can force the move by objecting at least 10 days before the hearing, so the defendant’s choice may decide the question for you.

How Long Collection Can Take Under the 10% Cap

Arizona’s garnishment limit changes the math on larger claims. Suppose you win a $4,000 judgment and the debtor has $800 a week in disposable earnings. The 10% cap allows about $80 a week to be garnished, so it would take roughly 50 weeks, plus interest and costs, to collect in full from wages alone. That is why many Arizona creditors also look for bank accounts, non-exempt property or real estate, and why a realistic settlement before the hearing can be worth more than a larger judgment.

Common Arizona Small Claims Cases

  • Security deposits. Within 14 days (excluding weekends and holidays) after the tenancy ends, the tenant returns possession and the tenant asks for it, the landlord must provide an itemized list of deductions and any refund due (A.R.S. §33-1321). Start with a security deposit demand letter.
  • Contractor and repair disputes. Contracts, payments, photos and a second estimate.
  • Personal loans. A promissory note, texts or bank transfers.
  • Consumer purchases. Receipts and messages showing the defect and the seller’s refusal.

Common Mistakes in Arizona Small Claims

  • Using the old $3,500 limit. Many guides still show it. The limit is now $5,000.
  • Assuming you can appeal. You cannot. Transfer the case in time if appeal rights matter to you.
  • Filing an assigned claim. Assignees and debt buyers cannot use small claims.
  • Counting on wage garnishment. The 10% cap makes collection from wages slow for larger judgments.

How Arizona Compares

Even after the 2025 increase, Arizona’s $5,000 limit is among the lower limits in the country, below Colorado ($7,500), Nevada ($10,000) and Utah ($20,000). Its no-appeal rule and lawyer ban are among the strictest. See every state in our small claims court limits by state table, or compare with Michigan, which also bars lawyers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the small claims limit in Arizona in 2026?

$5,000, exclusive of interest and costs. Senate Bill 1022 raised it from $3,500 in 2025.

How much does it cost to file small claims in Arizona?

In Maricopa County, $58 to file and $46 to answer, plus service costs. Other counties publish similar schedules.

Can I appeal an Arizona small claims decision?

No. The decision is final and binding. To keep appeal rights, transfer the case to the regular justice court at least 10 days before the hearing.

Can a lawyer represent me in Arizona small claims court?

No. Attorneys cannot take any part in filing, prosecuting or defending a small claim. Businesses appear through an officer, partner or authorized full-time employee.

How long does the defendant have to answer in Arizona?

20 days from service. Once an answer is filed, the hearing is set within 60 days.

How much of a debtor’s wages can be garnished in Arizona?

For ordinary debts, the lesser of 10% of disposable earnings or the amount above 60 times the applicable minimum wage per week.
